# ZigZag 0.1.8

Share a design to zigzag.diy from the app, and open a shared one from its
page; an account that keeps your machines and palettes in step between
computers; variable fonts at any weight; messages to the agent queued
while it works; and a long line of satin type that plans in a moment
instead of seconds.

## Sharing

- **File › Share…** (and the toolbar's button) puts a design on
  zigzag.diy: a page with its picture, a stitch player and downloads for
  every machine. No account is needed.
- Who can see it: anyone, anyone with the link (forever, or for a year, a
  month, a week, a day or an hour), or only you. Each choice takes effect
  as it's made.
- A license says what others may do with it (CC BY 4.0 unless you choose
  another), and Downloads chooses between the editable design with the
  machine files, or the machine files alone.
- After an edit the panel offers Update, which sends the design to the
  same page; Delete takes it off zigzag.diy.
- A copy made with Save As is its own design, with a page of its own. A
  file that's moved or renamed keeps its page.
- The same panel's Export tab lists a file for every machine, the
  document's machine first.

## Opening shared designs

- The Open button on a zigzag.diy page opens the design in ZigZag (a
  `zigzag://` link). It's saved in a ZigZag folder in Downloads, never
  over a file that's there.
- On Windows and Linux, start ZigZag once from where you keep it so the
  link knows where to find it (see the README in the download).

## Account

- **Settings › Account**: sign in to zigzag.diy with a code sent to your
  email. What this computer shared before goes into the account, and you
  get a screen name you can change.
- Signed in, your machines and their hoops, your thread palettes, the
  thread chart, how you last shared, and the last lettering font follow
  you to your other computers. The newest change wins; a change made
  offline goes up when the connection is back; and the first time a
  computer signs in, the machines and palettes it already has are kept
  beside the account's.

## Lettering

- Variable fonts are drawn at the weight chosen (and their other axes),
  not only at their default.
- The fonts a Mac keeps for its own interface (keyboard caps, clock
  numerals and the like) are left out of the font list.

## Working with an agent

- What you write while the agent works waits above the prompt and goes
  as one message when its turn ends. Take one back with its ×; Stop puts
  them back in the prompt.
- An empty chat starts with three ideas, one of them a change to the
  design that's open.
- The floating Agent panel waits in the canvas's lower left corner and
  drags taller or shorter by its top edge. Put away, the tool strip's
  Agent button fills while the agent works and shows a dot for a reply
  not yet read.
- The thread chart (Brother or Janome) is remembered.

## Agents

- In a `batch`, `$N.name` is the name of the one object step N added,
  whether or not the step answered tersely.
- A short-answer edit to a big design is planned in the background: the
  window keeps drawing, and the answer says what the edit broke once the
  plan is back.

## Fixes

- A long line of satin lettering (dozens of columns) no longer takes
  seconds to plan: the search for the sewing order stops when further
  tries stop helping.
- Floating panels sent to the corner no longer land exactly on top of
  each other, and a floating Agent panel made tall on a big screen stays
  inside a smaller window.
- Palettes that change while one is being renamed no longer crash the app.
